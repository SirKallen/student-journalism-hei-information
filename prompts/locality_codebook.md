# Locality Binary Codebook

## Inclusion Criteria

Code an article as **locality = 1** only when it passes all three gates below.

If the article fails any gate, code it as **locality = 0**. If the local and non-local frames appear balanced or unclear, flag the article for review.

A local article does not need to center only one local unit. Articles may be coded as **locality = 1** when they focus on multiple qualifying local units, such as several towns, cities, neighborhoods, campuses, or local institutions.

The decisive question is whether the article’s primary geographic scale is local.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article’s primary frame is county-level, regional, state-level, national, international, or non-geographic, even if it includes several local examples.

## Gate 1: Local Unit Gate

The article must center one or more specific local units.

A local article may focus on multiple towns, cities, neighborhoods, campuses, or local institutions, as long as the article’s primary geographic frame remains local rather than county-level, regional, state-level, national, international, or non-geographic.

### Qualifying Local Units

- a specific town;
- a specific city;
- a municipality;
- a village;
- a neighborhood;
- a district within a city;
- a specific campus community;
- a specific local institution;
- a consolidated city-county;
- an island municipality;
- a specific K–12 school, school district, local education agency, or local public education system.

### Non-Local Units

- county-level coverage;
- regional coverage;
- state-level coverage;
- national coverage;
- international coverage;
- non-geographic coverage.

An article may mention multiple local units. It may also mention both local and non-local units.

The article may be coded as local when its main focus is substantively related to one or more qualifying local units. No single local unit needs to dominate if the article’s primary geographic scale is still local.

County-level coverage is usually non-local, but a county-equivalent jurisdiction may be treated as local when the article centers a municipal/local government action, local public service, or community issue within that jurisdiction rather than a broad regional or statewide pattern.

If the article is primarily focused on a non-local unit, code **locality = 0**.

## Gate 2: Local Topic Orientation Gate

The article’s primary topic must be substantively oriented around the specific local unit or units identified in Gate 1.

Qualifying local topics may include:

- a local government decision;
- a campus policy or campus event;
- a local election or public meeting;
- a local protest or community conflict;
- a local crime or public risk;
- a local business or organization;
- a local athletic or community event;
- a local housing, transportation, labor, environmental, educational, or public-service issue;
- the local effects of a broader state, national, or international issue;
- a local or campus-centered trend story;
- a local or campus-centered profile, opinion piece, sports story, arts story, lifestyle story, or feature.

The article does not pass this gate if the local unit appears only as background, setting, source affiliation, dateline, or incidental context.

The local unit must matter to the substance of the article.

If the article’s topic is not substantively oriented around the local unit, code **locality = 0**.

## Gate 3: Primary Focus Gate

The local unit or units must be the article’s dominant geographic frame.

A single local unit does not need to dominate. Multiple local units may collectively form the article’s primary frame if the article is mainly about local communities, local institutions, local events, or local effects.

The article passes this gate when the main subject cannot be accurately summarized without reference to the specific local unit or units, or without reference to local communities as the article’s primary geographic scale.

If the article would mean essentially the same thing without the local unit, the local element is not sufficient.

A local unit may be central when it defines at least one of the following:

- where the main event occurred;
- who is affected;
- which institution is acting;
- what decision is being reported;
- what issue is being reported;
- what community, campus, organization, person, event, or experience the article is mainly about.

The article does not pass this gate if it mainly focuses on a county, region, state, national issue, international issue, general trend, cultural topic, or broad public issue while using a local unit only as an example.

If both local and non-local frames are present:

- If the dominant frame is local, the article passes this gate.
- If the dominant frame is non-local, the article fails this gate.
- If the local and non-local frames appear balanced, flag the article for review.

# Clarification Rules for Specific Article Types

## Locality Does Not Require Civic Importance

This variable measures whether the article is geographically local, not whether the article addresses a civic or public-information need.

Do not require the article to be about government, policy, public affairs, or community problems.

Sports stories, arts reviews, lifestyle features, profiles, business stories, event previews, and opinion articles may be coded as **locality = 1** if they pass all three locality gates.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article’s primary subject is a specific local unit, even if the topic is not civic or policy-oriented.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article’s primary subject is not oriented around a specific local unit.

## Campus Community as a Local Unit

A specific campus community qualifies as a local unit.

Campus-focused articles may be coded as **locality = 1** when the article’s primary topic is substantively about that specific campus community, campus institution, campus event, campus population, campus policy, campus space, or campus experience.

This includes campus lectures, speaker series, visiting speakers, academic panels, workshops, performances, debates, and public events when the article primarily reports on the event as part of the campus community.

The surrounding town does not need to be named if the article clearly centers a specific campus community.

Code as **locality = 0** when a campus, university, student, professor, administrator, or campus organization is mentioned only incidentally.

A university source, campus location, or student reaction is not sufficient by itself. The campus community must be the article’s dominant frame.

## Specific Local Institutions

A specific local institution may qualify as a local unit when the institution itself is central to the article.

Local institutions may include schools, school districts, local education agencies, libraries, hospitals, city halls, local businesses, nonprofits, churches, museums, venues, community organizations, campus offices, campus departments, or campus facilities.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article is primarily about the institution, its actions, its role in the local/campus community, or an event or issue centered on that institution.

Code as **locality = 0** when the institution is only named as a source affiliation, employer, background detail, or setting.

## Local Sources, Students, Faculty, and Expert Commentary

A local source does not make an article local by itself.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article quotes or profiles a local person, student, professor, official, resident, business owner, or organization but the article’s primary topic is not oriented around the local unit.

Faculty or expert commentary is local only when the article primarily connects the expertise to a specific campus or local community.

Code as **locality = 1** when the local source’s role, activity, experience, or relationship to the specific local unit is central to the article.

Code as **locality = 0** when the local source only comments on a broader state, national, international, or non-geographic issue.

## Profiles

Profiles are local only when the person’s role, activity, experience, or significance within a specific campus or local community is central to the article.

Code as **locality = 1** when the profile is primarily about a person as a campus leader, local resident, local artist, local athlete, local business owner, campus organizer, faculty member, staff member, student activist, local official, or community member within a specific local unit.

Code as **locality = 0** when the profile is mainly biographical, celebrity-focused, nationally oriented, or focused on personal achievement without making a specific campus or local community central.

## Opinion, Editorials, Columns, and Commentary

Opinion format does not determine locality.

Code opinion articles as **locality = 1** only when the argument is primarily about a specific campus, town, city, neighborhood, district, local institution, or local community.

Code opinion articles as **locality = 0** when they primarily address state, national, international, cultural, moral, lifestyle, or general issues without centering a specific local unit.

A local author or local publication does not make an opinion article local. The argument itself must be locally oriented.

## Arts, Culture, Entertainment, Lifestyle, and Business Coverage

Arts, culture, entertainment, lifestyle, and business articles can be local.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article primarily covers a campus or local event, venue, artist, performance, exhibit, restaurant, business, festival, cultural institution, student production, or local cultural activity.

This includes profiles of local artists, performers, writers, or cultural workers whose work addresses broader cultural, political, national, international, or identity-based themes, as long as the article primarily centers the artist’s local practice, local venue, local exhibition, or role in a specific local arts community.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article primarily reviews or discusses national entertainment, celebrities, music, film, television, books, fashion, lifestyle, internet culture, or general cultural trends without a specific local or campus frame.

A local restaurant review, campus theater review, local concert preview, student art feature, or local business profile can be local if the article’s primary subject is the local unit.

## Sports Coverage

Sports articles can be local.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article primarily covers a specific campus team, local team, campus athlete, local athlete, game, match, rivalry, athletic event, facility, coach, or sports issue involving a specific campus or local community.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article primarily covers national sports, professional sports, international sports, general sports commentary, rankings, or athletes without a specific campus or local focus.

Ordinary campus sports recaps count as local if they center a specific campus team, game, athlete, or athletic event.

## Student Government and Campus Governance

Student government articles are local only when they focus on governance, elections, policies, meetings, decisions, campaigns, candidates, or actions within a specific campus community.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article centers a specific campus student government, campus election, student senate meeting, student policy debate, or student-government decision.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article primarily discusses national student organizations, state student coalitions, general youth politics, or student political trends without centering a specific campus community.

## Internal Campus and Institutional News

Internal campus or institutional news may be local when the specific campus institution is the article’s primary subject.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article primarily focuses on a specific campus, campus department, campus office, campus facility, campus program, campus policy, campus service, campus leadership decision, campus event, or campus population.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article only uses the campus as a source affiliation or incidental setting.

Articles about admissions, rankings, awards, fundraising, leadership changes, program openings, program closures, or institutional announcements may be local if the article primarily centers the specific campus institution.

## Multi-Campus University Systems

Multi-campus system coverage is non-local when the university system, statewide higher education system, or system-level policy is the dominant frame.

However, articles comparing or reporting on multiple specific campuses may be coded as **locality = 1** when the campuses themselves are the primary units of analysis and the article remains grounded in campus-level effects, events, populations, or institutions.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article primarily focuses on a university system, statewide higher education system, or multiple campuses without one specific campus as the main focus.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article primarily explains how a system-level issue affects one specific campus community, campus institution, or campus population.

If the article gives roughly equal attention to multiple campuses and no single campus dominates, code **locality = 0** or flag for review if unclear.

## Research, Studies, and Academic Expertise

Research stories are local only when the research is primarily about, conducted in, or applied to a specific campus or local community.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article connects the research to a specific campus, town, city, neighborhood, local institution, or local population as the main focus.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article mainly presents general research findings, expert analysis, scientific information, or academic publicity without a specific local or campus-centered frame.

## Locally Grounded Trend Stories

Articles about broader state, national, international, or cultural trends may be coded as **locality = 1** when the article develops the trend primarily through specific local units, campus communities, or local institutions.

A trend story may pass the locality gates when local actors, local institutions, or campus communities are not merely quoted as examples, but provide the main evidence, narrative structure, and interpretive basis for the article’s claims.

Code as **locality = 1** when most of the article’s evidence, scenes, sources, examples, or narrative development come from specific local or campus communities.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article’s broader claim depends substantially on local or campus-based cases.

Code as **locality = 0** when local people or places appear only as brief examples, reactions, or illustrations of a primarily state, national, international, or non-geographic story.

If local sources are used only to illustrate a broader claim already established by state, national, or international data, code **locality = 0**.

If local sources or local cases are used to build the article’s main claim, code **locality = 1**.

If the article develops both a local/campus frame and a broader trend frame and neither is clearly dominant, flag for review.

## State, National, or International Issues With Local Effects

Articles about broader state, national, or international issues may be local if the dominant frame is a specific local unit.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article primarily explains concrete effects on a specific campus, town, city, neighborhood, local institution, or local population.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article primarily summarizes the broader issue and only briefly includes local reactions, local quotes, or local examples.

Policy stories are local when the article primarily explains concrete effects on a specific campus or local community, not merely reactions to the policy.

## Campus Events Connected to Broader Issues

Campus events tied to state, national, international, or cultural issues may be local when the article primarily covers the campus event, organizers, participants, campus response, or campus community.

The topic discussed at a campus event may be state-level, national, international, academic, cultural, or non-geographic.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article’s dominant frame is that the event occurred at a specific campus and involved that campus community.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article mainly reports on the campus event or campus community response.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article mainly summarizes the broader state, national, international, or cultural issue and includes the campus event only briefly.

## County, Regional, and Multi-Community Coverage

County-level, regional, and multi-community articles are generally non-local under this codebook.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article primarily focuses on a county, region, multiple towns, multiple counties, or a broad geographic area without one dominant local unit.

County institutions do not qualify by themselves. However, code as **locality = 1** if the article’s dominant frame is the effect of a county action on a specific town, campus, neighborhood, or local institution.

Articles that compare several towns, summarize a regional trend, or describe a multi-community issue should be coded as non-local unless one specific local unit is clearly the article’s main focus.

Do not automatically code a story as non-local because it uses an island-wide, metro-wide, or consolidated city-county frame.

If the article is mainly about local governance, local public services, local safety, or local community conflict within that jurisdiction, it may be coded as **locality = 1**.

## Virtual Events

Virtual events may be local if the hosting organization, participants, audience, issue, or event purpose is primarily tied to a specific campus or local unit.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article primarily covers a virtual event as an event of a specific campus community, local institution, town, neighborhood, or local organization.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article primarily covers a general online event, national webinar, virtual panel, livestream, or digital trend without a specific local or campus-centered frame.

## Datelines, Metadata, and Publication Context

Datelines, publication location, outlet identity, URLs, section labels, tags, author affiliation, archive labels, and metadata do not establish locality.

The article body must substantively center the local unit.

Code as **locality = 0** when the only local evidence comes from metadata, dateline, outlet identity, or source affiliation rather than the article’s substantive content.

## Consolidated Local Jurisdictions, Islands, and Metro-Wide Local Issues

Some local issues are governed or experienced through jurisdictions larger than a single town or neighborhood, such as consolidated city-counties, island municipalities, boroughs, parishes, or metro-wide local governments.

Code as **locality = 1** when the article primarily centers local governance, local public services, local public safety, local transportation, local enforcement, or local community conflict within such a jurisdiction.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article treats the jurisdiction mainly as a county, region, statewide area, or broad geographic example and does not center local governance, local institutions, or local community effects.

A national or statewide trend mentioned for context does not make the article non-local if the dominant frame remains a specific local jurisdiction or local communities.

## Campus Lectures, Speaker Series, Panels, and Academic Talks

Code as **locality = 1** when an article primarily covers a lecture, speaker series, panel, workshop, debate, performance, or public event hosted by a specific campus or local institution.

The subject of the event may be state-level, national, international, academic, cultural, or non-geographic.

The article may still be local if the dominant frame is the campus/local event, including the host institution, audience, attendees, Q&A, organizers, or campus context.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article primarily summarizes the speaker’s research, book, political views, national issue, or expert commentary and mentions the campus event only briefly as background or setting.

## First-Person Articles and Personal Narratives

First-person format does not determine locality.

Code a first-person article as **locality = 1** when the writer’s personal experience is primarily organized around one or more specific local units, such as a campus community, local institution, local event, local team, neighborhood, city, town, or local organization.

Code a first-person article as **locality = 0** when the article is mainly a personal reflection, advice piece, lifestyle essay, opinion essay, memoir-style piece, or general commentary without a substantive local or campus-centered frame.

A first-person article should not be coded as local merely because the writer is a student, lives in a local area, attends a university, or mentions a local place in passing.

The local unit must matter to the substance of the article.

For first-person articles, ask:

> Would the article lose its main meaning if the campus, local institution, local event, local team, town, city, or neighborhood were removed?

If yes, the local frame is substantive. If no, the local reference is incidental.

# Exclusion Criteria

Code an article as **locality = 0** when it does not pass all three inclusion gates.

An article should not be coded as local merely because it mentions a local place, quotes a local person, appears in a student-produced news outlet, or includes a campus or town reference.

The article must center a local unit, orient its primary topic around that unit, and make that unit the dominant frame of the article.

## Non-Local Geographic Scale

Code an article as **locality = 0** when its primary geographic focus is broader than a local unit.

Non-local geographic scales include:

- county-level coverage;
- regional coverage;
- state-level coverage;
- national coverage;
- international coverage;
- non-geographic coverage.

Examples include articles primarily about a county government decision, a statewide policy debate, a national election, an international conflict, or a general cultural issue.

These articles should be coded as non-local unless the article’s dominant frame is the effect of the issue on a specific local unit.

## County-Level Coverage

Code an article as **locality = 0** when the article primarily focuses on a county-level issue, institution, policy, population, or event.

County-level articles do not count as local under this codebook, even if they include towns, cities, campuses, or neighborhoods within the county.

A county article may be flagged for review if it includes a specific local unit and it is unclear whether the article’s dominant frame is the county or the local unit.

## Regional or Multi-Community Coverage

Code an article as **locality = 0** when the article primarily focuses on a region, county, multiple counties, or broad geographic area rather than on qualifying local units.

Do not code an article as non-local merely because it mentions multiple towns, cities, neighborhoods, campuses, or local institutions.

Multiple local units may still qualify as local if they are treated as specific local communities rather than as an undifferentiated region.

Articles that compare several towns, cities, neighborhoods, campuses, or local institutions may be coded as **locality = 1** when the comparison remains focused on those specific local units.

Code as **locality = 0** when the article abstracts those places into a county, region, statewide pattern, or broad geographic area, or when the local units function only as examples of a broader non-local issue.

## State-Level Coverage

Code an article as **locality = 0** when the article primarily focuses on state government, state agencies, state policy, state elections, statewide institutions, or statewide public issues.

State-level coverage should not be coded as local merely because it mentions a university, city, town, local official, or local source.

To be coded as local, the article must primarily explain the effects of the state issue on a specific local unit.

## National or Federal Coverage

Code an article as **locality = 0** when the article primarily focuses on national politics, the federal government, federal policy, national organizations, national culture, national trends, or national public issues.

A local quote, local reaction, or local expert source is not sufficient to make the article local.

The article must primarily focus on a specific local unit or campus to be coded as **locality = 1**.

## International Coverage

Code an article as **locality = 0** when the article primarily focuses on another country, global affairs, international conflict, international organizations, or global issues.

An international article should not be coded as local merely because it includes local reactions, campus commentary, or a local event reference.

It should be coded as local only if the dominant frame is a specific campus, town, neighborhood, or local event connected to the international issue.

## Non-Geographic or General Coverage

Code an article as **locality = 0** when the article has no clear geographic focus.

This includes general articles about lifestyle, entertainment, culture, opinion, advice, science, technology, sports, or personal experience when the article is not primarily oriented around a specific local unit.

## Passing Mentions of a Local Unit

Code an article as **locality = 0** when a local unit appears only as a passing mention, background detail, setting, dateline, source location, or incidental example.

The local unit must matter to the substance of the article.

If the article would mean essentially the same thing without the local unit, the article should be coded as non-local.

## Local Source Without Local Focus

Code an article as **locality = 0** when the article quotes or profiles a local person, student, professor, official, resident, business owner, or organization but the article’s primary topic is not oriented around the local unit.

A local source does not make an article local unless the local source’s role in the specific local community is central to the article.

## Campus Mention Without Campus Focus

Code an article as **locality = 0** when a campus, university, student, professor, administrator, or campus organization is mentioned only incidentally.

Campus-focused articles may count as local, but the campus community must be the article’s dominant frame.

A university source, campus location, or student reaction is not sufficient by itself.

## Broader Issue With Weak Local Connection

Code an article as **locality = 0** when the article primarily discusses a state, national, international, or broad public issue and only briefly mentions local effects.

The article should be coded as local only when the specific local unit is central to the article’s main subject.

If local consequences are vague, undeveloped, or secondary, code the article as non-local or flag it for review.
