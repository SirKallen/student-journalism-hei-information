# CIN Binary Codebook

## Inclusion Criteria

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 1** only when it passes both of the following gates:

### Gate 1: CIN Topic Gate

The article must substantively address at least one recognized Critical Information Need topic as one of its main points.

Recognized CIN topics include:

1. **Emergencies, risks, and public safety**  
   Immediate threats, long-term risks, crime and safety patterns, emergency preparedness, policing, courts, public warnings, environmental hazards, and safety-related public services.

2. **Health and welfare**  
   Public health conditions, health-care access, mental health services, food insecurity, housing instability, disability access, childcare, social services, and group-specific health or welfare needs when connected to a public or community need.

3. **Education and educational access**  
   K–12 education, higher education policy, affordability, tuition, financial aid, admissions access, workforce training, public funding, program closures or expansions, and educational choices available to students, families, workers, or community members.

4. **Transportation and mobility**  
   Transit options, road access, parking policy when connected to broader access or mobility issues, costs, schedules, infrastructure, pedestrian and bicycle safety, disability access, and mobility barriers.

5. **Economic opportunity and labor**  
   Jobs, wages, labor conditions, unionization, worker rights, workforce development, job training, small business support, public spending, local or regional economic effects, and the HEI’s role as an employer or economic actor.

6. **Environment and land use**  
   Air and water quality, climate risks, environmental hazards, sustainability with public consequences, land use, development, public access to parks or natural resources, and environmental effects on surrounding communities.

7. **Civic information and public participation**  
   Public institutions, public meetings, voting access, civic participation, public records, community organizations, public services, legal rights, opportunities for association, and opportunities for public action.

8. **Political and policy information**  
   Candidates, elections, ballot issues, public governance, state or local policy decisions, public budgets, public accountability, rights, and policy decisions affecting communities, neighborhoods, institutions, public resources, or affected groups.

A CIN topic is substantive only when the article develops the issue enough that it could be summarized as one of the article’s main points. Passing mentions, isolated quotes, slogans, background details, event themes, or brief references to CIN-related topics are not sufficient.

### Gate 2: Local/Public Information Gate

The article must provide substantive information relevant to a public community or public consequence beyond purely internal campus life.

Relevant public communities may include:

- the surrounding town or city;
- a town/rural community;
- an urban neighborhood or metropolitan community;
- the county or region;
- the state;
- residents, workers, families, voters, tenants, patients, students, parents, public agencies, civic organizations, businesses, or other public constituencies;
- the HEI as a major public, economic, health, educational, land-use, or civic institution when its actions affect a broader public community.

Campus-related articles may count only when they connect campus actors, HEI institutions, students, or student-produced news outlets to a broader public information need. The article must do more than show that the issue is important to students or campus life.

Students may be treated as a public constituency when the article addresses them as tenants, workers, voters, patients, transit users, consumers, financial-aid recipients, residents, parents, or rights-bearing members of the public. Students do not qualify as a public constituency merely because the article concerns student preferences, campus culture, clubs, entertainment, athletics, or ordinary student life.

A campus connection is neither required nor sufficient. The article must substantively inform readers about a recognized CIN topic and a public community, public consequence, public decision, public resource, public risk, or public action.

## Substantiveness Standard

To be coded as **CIN_binary = 1**, the article should help readers understand at least one of the following:

- why the issue matters;
- who is affected;
- what choices, policies, risks, services, rights, or decisions are involved;
- what options, resources, actions, or public processes are available;
- how the HEI, local government, public agencies, civic organizations, employers, or community actors are involved;
- what consequences the issue has for a town/rural community, urban community, county, region, state, or broader public community.

## Briefs, Updates, and Follow-Up Items

Code each article based on the substantive information contained in the article being coded.

A brief update, closure notice, reminder, or follow-up item should be coded as **CIN_binary = 1** only if the article itself provides enough public information to satisfy both the CIN Topic Gate and the Local/Public Information Gate.

A link or reference to prior reporting does not by itself make the article CIN unless the project’s coding protocol explicitly instructs coders to incorporate linked context.

## Special Content Types

### Sports

Sports articles may count as CIN only if they substantively address a public or institutional issue such as public funding, labor, athlete rights, Title IX, safety, discrimination, facilities access, transportation, local economic effects, land use, or governance.

### Arts, Culture, and Entertainment

Arts and culture articles may count as CIN only if they substantively address public funding, censorship, labor conditions, accessibility, equity, community impact, institutional policy, local public debate, or cultural access as a public issue.

### Events, Previews, and Announcements

Event articles, previews, and announcements may count as CIN only if the article provides substantive information about the underlying public issue, resource, policy, service, risk, right, or community need.

Event logistics, publicity, or awareness language alone are not sufficient.

### Profiles

Profile articles may count as CIN only if the profile is used to substantially inform readers about a public, civic, institutional, health, safety, economic, housing, educational, environmental, transportation, rights/access, or policy issue.

### Opinion, Editorials, Columns, and Advocacy Articles

Opinion format is not disqualifying.

Opinion pieces, editorials, columns, essays, and advocacy articles may be coded as CIN when they provide substantive public information about a recognized CIN topic and connect that topic to a local, regional, state, or broader public community.

To count, the article must do more than express a viewpoint. It should explain public decisions, policy proposals, legal rights, institutional responsibilities, affected groups, public resources, risks, consequences, available services, or civic actions.

Pure commentary, personal reflection, unsupported opinion, moral judgment, or cultural interpretation does not count.

### State Policy Stories Affecting an HEI

State policy stories affecting an HEI may count as CIN when the article explains consequences for public access, tuition, affordability, admissions, financial aid, regional employment, workforce training, public services, local communities, students as state residents, families, workers, or state taxpayers.

State policy stories should not be coded as CIN when they only describe internal HEI administration, budgeting, leadership strategy, institutional inconvenience, or planning without explaining broader public consequences.

Flag for review when the policy clearly could affect public access or public resources, but the article gives only vague or undeveloped consequences.

### Local Projects, Institutional Initiatives, and Development Stories

Articles about construction, renovation, expansion, openings, closures, business developments, institutional initiatives, public-facing facilities, or local projects may count as CIN only when they substantively explain a recognized public information need.

Relevant public information may include effects on access, affordability, public spending, public services, land use, transportation, housing, jobs, labor conditions, safety, rights, environmental conditions, governance, or affected community groups.

The article must develop those public consequences as one of its main points.

### Temporary Closures, Outages, and Service Disruptions

Articles about temporary closures, outages, reduced hours, service interruptions, or access disruptions may count as CIN when they substantively explain effects on public access, health, safety, transportation, education, public services, rights, costs, available alternatives, affected groups, or policy causes.

Code as **CIN_binary = 0** when the article mainly announces the disruption without explaining broader public consequences or available resources.

### Research and Expert Commentary

Articles about HEI research, faculty expertise, studies, reports, or academic projects may count as CIN only when they translate the information into public risks, public services, policy implications, local or regional conditions, available resources, affected groups, or actions relevant to a public community.

Research publicity alone is not sufficient.

### Educational Program and Workforce-Training Features Without Public-Information Substance

Code as 0 when an article primarily profiles, promotes, or describes an HEI academic program, course, lab, certificate, major, internship, training activity, or hands-on learning opportunity, even if the article mentions career preparation, employability, job skills, workforce needs, or future employment.

Mentions that students are being trained for careers or jobs are not sufficient by themselves to qualify as economic opportunity, labor, or education CIN.

To code as 1, the article must substantively connect the program to a broader public information need, such as regional workforce shortages, public funding, access to training for non-students, credential pathways, employment outcomes, industry demand, public-private workforce initiatives, barriers to participation, or effects on the surrounding community, labor market, or public services.

# Exclusion Criteria

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 0** when it does not provide substantive information about a recognized Critical Information Need affecting a town/rural community, urban community, surrounding town or city, county, region, state, or another public community beyond purely internal campus life.

Do not code an article as CIN merely because it mentions students, a university, a campus, a local place, a public event, a student organization, a government actor, a public controversy, or a topic that could be related to a Critical Information Need.

The article must develop the CIN issue enough that it could be summarized as one of the article’s main points.

When an article plausibly relates to a CIN topic but does not clearly provide substantive public information, coders should use the project’s uncertainty or review flag rather than stretching the definition of CIN.

## Campus-Only Articles

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 0** when it focuses only on internal campus activities, student experiences, student organizations, campus traditions, residence life, course experiences, departmental updates, athletics, entertainment, or university events without connecting them to a broader local, regional, state, or public information need.

## Internal HEI Administration

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 0** when it concerns internal HEI administration, staffing, budgeting, programming, policies, leadership changes, rankings, awards, or operations without explaining consequences for public access, local communities, regional employment, state residents, public services, rights, safety, affordability, educational access, or other CIN-related public needs.

## Ordinary Sports Coverage

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 0** when it primarily reports game results, athlete performance, team rankings, schedules, previews, recaps, recruiting, coach/player profiles, or fan reaction without addressing a broader public or institutional issue.

## Arts, Culture, Entertainment, and Lifestyle Coverage

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 0** when it primarily reviews, previews, or describes performances, concerts, films, exhibits, books, food, fashion, music, restaurants, cultural events, entertainment, or lifestyle topics without addressing a broader public-information issue.

## Event Logistics, Promotion, or Publicity

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 0** when it mainly provides logistics, promotion, or publicity for an event, such as time, date, location, speakers, activities, registration details, ticket information, or general announcements, without explaining the underlying public issue, resource, policy, service, risk, right, or community need.

An awareness event, fundraiser, lecture, panel, charity drive, or volunteer activity does not count as CIN unless the article itself explains the public problem, affected groups, relevant services, resources, policy stakes, or available actions.

## Profiles Without Public-Information Substance

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 0** when it focuses on an individual’s biography, personality, achievements, hobbies, career path, creative work, personal values, or personal story without using that profile to explain a broader local, regional, state, or public information need.

## Passing Mentions of CIN Topics

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 0** when a CIN topic appears only as a passing mention, background detail, quote fragment, slogan, event theme, anecdote, or minor context.

Brief references to public safety, health, education, transportation, housing, labor, politics, policy, equity, environment, or civic life are not sufficient by themselves.

## Opinion, Editorial, and Column Articles Without Public-Information Substance

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 0** when it is an opinion piece, editorial, column, essay, review, or reflection that mainly expresses a viewpoint, complaint, endorsement, personal experience, moral judgment, emotional reaction, or cultural interpretation without providing substantive public information about a recognized CIN topic.

A reader should be able to learn concrete public information from the article even if they disagree with the author’s opinion.

If the article only persuades, reacts, praises, condemns, or reflects, code it as non-CIN.

## Public Controversy Commentary

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 0** when it primarily comments on, interprets, defends, criticizes, or reflects on a public controversy without providing substantive public information about the underlying issue.

This includes articles that focus mainly on personal opinion, moral judgment, intent, civility, offense, forgiveness, backlash, public reaction, symbolism, or cultural interpretation, even when the controversy involves a public official, public institution, public meeting, political issue, social conflict, or rights-related topic.

A public controversy alone is not sufficient for CIN.

To be coded as CIN, the article must develop the underlying public issue enough to inform readers about concrete public consequences, affected groups, policy decisions, institutional actions, accountability processes, legal or civic implications, public resources, or available actions.

## State, National, or Public Policy Mentions Without Public Consequences

Code an article as **CIN_binary = 0** when it mentions state, national, or public policy but does not explain consequences for communities, residents, students as public-policy subjects, families, workers, public services, educational access, affordability, rights, safety, public resources, or other CIN-related public needs.

For HEI-related policy stories, code **CIN_binary = 0** when the article only discusses internal university strategy, administration, budgeting, leadership response, or institutional planning without explaining broader public consequences.

## Public Symbols and Historical-Memory Controversies

Articles about flags, monuments, public names, holidays, commemorations, mascots, or other civic symbols may count as CIN only when they substantively inform readers about public institutions, government decisions, official policy, civic identity, historical interpretation, affected public communities, legal or political implications, or opportunities for public action.

Code as **CIN_binary = 0** when the article primarily offers cultural reflection, personal reaction, symbolic interpretation, or controversy commentary without explaining concrete civic, governmental, institutional, policy, rights, or public-community implications.

## Service Journalism and How-To Articles

Code how-to, advice, or practical guidance articles as **CIN_binary = 0** when they provide general lifestyle advice, consumer tips, hobbies, entertainment guidance, personal productivity advice, or personal-interest information without a substantive public-information issue.

Such articles may count only when they substantively inform readers about a recognized CIN topic and connect that guidance to local public conditions, risks, services, resources, rights, policies, environmental constraints, or community needs.

## Campus-Community Engagement Without a Public Need

Articles about university groups, clubs, teams, departments, or programs engaging with local residents should be coded as **CIN_binary = 0** when they primarily describe outreach, performance, volunteering, community bonding, local pride, enrichment, public relations, or town-gown connection without developing a recognized public-information issue.

Campus-community engagement is not sufficient by itself.

The article must substantively explain a public need such as access to resources, affordability, equity, health or safety, education access, labor, local economic effects, public funding, governance, transportation, environmental impact, rights, or policy decisions.
